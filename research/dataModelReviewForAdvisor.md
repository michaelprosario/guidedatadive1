# GUIDE v1.0.0 Schema Review for Advisor Needs

## Purpose and scope

This review compares the GUIDE logical data model in `2026-11 DevFest Florida/GUIDE Materials/guide_data_model_schema_v1.0.0.json` with the advisor pain points in `research/commonAdvisorPainPoints.md`. It identifies data elements that could support advisor workflows; it does not establish that an institution has implemented, populated, or validated those elements. Data completeness, source-system integration, policy configuration, and staff practice determine whether the model produces useful day-to-day guidance.

## High-value model elements

| Advisor pain point | Relevant GUIDE elements | Potential advisor value and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confusing requirements and inefficient course plans | `ProgramRequirement` and `ProgramRequirementCourse` describe curriculum requirements, hierarchy, credit/course minimums, and associated courses. `PersonProgramProgressEvaluation`, `PersonProgramRequirementEvaluation`, and `PersonProgramRequirementEvidence` record an authoritative evaluation against a specific curriculum, including satisfaction status, credits/courses satisfied, exceptions, and evidence. `LearnerDegreePlan` and revision-linked `LearnerDegreePlanItem` connect requirements and courses to planned terms. `LearnerDegreePlanPreferences` and `LearnerDegreePlanAvailability` capture term-hour limits, summer intent, campus/modality preferences, and availability windows. | Supports a requirement-by-requirement progress discussion and a learner-specific term plan that accounts for preferences and recognized credit. The schema explicitly treats `ProgramRequirement` as descriptive rather than a universal degree-audit rules language; use the institution's authoritative evaluation for what is actually satisfied. Preferences and availability also do not guarantee that a suitable section is offered or open. |
| Transfer-credit loss and articulation complexity | `LearnerTransferCreditEvaluation` records the receiving institution's decision and outcome. Its source-course and equivalent-course associations preserve what was evaluated and what receiving courses were recognized. `LearnerAcademicCredit` represents credit actually recognized by the institution; `LearnerAcademicCreditTransferEvaluation` preserves its transfer-evaluation provenance. Requirement evidence can then show where recognized credit was applied. | Makes the distinction between external work, an articulation decision, recognized credit, and credit applied to a program visible. Advisors can investigate unrecognized or unapplied work without treating every transcript course as awarded credit. The model enables this traceability; it does not itself reconcile inconsistent articulation policy or quantify credit loss unless institutions define those reports. |
| Registration holds and scheduling barriers | `PersonRegistrationEligibility` records a person's eligibility result and registration window for a term. `PersonRegistrationIneligibilityReason` carries source-reported reasons. `PersonServiceRestriction` records governed reasons, effective dates/terms, release dates, and responsible units; `PersonServiceRestrictionImpact` records the specific service or process affected. | Helps distinguish an eligibility result from a restriction and its reported impact, supporting timely referrals to the office that can resolve a block. A source-reported reason is not necessarily the authoritative underlying cause. The model does not imply a financial balance or that a specific debt caused a block; that requires an appropriate finance source and verified linkage. |
| High caseloads and reactive outreach | `PersonSupportAssignment` links the person receiving support to the support provider, role, primary-within-context indicator, and effective dates. `PersonCaseloadCommunication` records sender, trigger, contact method, sent date, message, and topic. `PersonCommunicationCampaign` supports targeted communication by learner or cohort, program, and term, with cadence, message, engagement counts, and a success-rate field. `LearnerEarlyAlert` and `LearnerEarlyAlertActivity` preserve alerts and follow-up activity. | Enables an advisor or program team to see current support assignments, outreach history, and whether a contact or campaign was followed by an outcome. These are useful foundations for proactive follow-up. They do not define a target caseload, advisor capacity, contact-time budget, or required high-touch frequency; institutions must establish those operational measures and define campaign success consistently. |
| Career and major uncertainty | `CareerPathway`, ordered/hierarchical `CareerPathwayComponent`, and `CareerPathwayJob` describe education-to-work routes, milestones, expected duration, occupations, and industries. Skill and strength associations add pathway context. | Can connect program and course planning to visible career destinations rather than treating course selection as an isolated audit. Pathway definitions alone do not prove that a particular learner has chosen or is progressing on a pathway; capture and maintain that learner-to-goal connection in the advising workflow. |
| Basic-needs barriers affecting persistence | `PersonChallenge` can preserve intake-reported challenges. `PersonSupportReferral` links a person to a resource, provider, or organization and can connect the referral to an alert activity or challenge, with status, reason, outcome, and close date. `PersonHousing`, `HousingRequest`, `PersonTransportation`, and financial-aid application/award records provide related context. | Supports coordinated, closed-loop referrals instead of leaving an advisor with an alert but no way to track next steps. These records are sensitive and may be incomplete or stale; access, consent, refresh cadence, and visibility should be governed. Financial-aid records should not be treated as a complete measure of hardship, and a housing or transportation record should not be assumed to represent every basic-needs gap. |
| Predictive early alerts, including first-term and bias concerns | `SuccessPredictionModel` records the model name, target outcome, version, time horizon, and description. `LearnerSuccessPrediction` stores the timestamped score, optional probability, and category. | Makes score meaning and model version more explicit than an unexplained risk label. The reviewed prediction fields do not provide model feature provenance, validation/calibration results, subgroup performance, or fairness monitoring. First-term learners may have limited institutional history, so a score should not be the sole basis for outreach or resource allocation. Use human review, measure outcomes, and monitor error rates and calibration across relevant groups and entry stages. |

## Systems-engineering assessment

The model's strongest fit is a connected advising view: program and curriculum context, authoritative progress and evidence, a revision-aware future plan, registration constraints, support assignments, and follow-up/referral history. This can reduce the time advisors spend reconciling disconnected academic and support records and make it easier to act before a student reaches a crisis point.

The main limitation is that a schema describes possible records and relationships, not an end-to-end advising service. In particular, degree-audit interpretation, available-section optimization, restriction resolution, advisor capacity planning, and equitable intervention policy still require institutional rules and operational ownership. The schema's tenant-scoped identifiers, source-system lineage, effective dates, and audit fields are useful controls, but integrations must preserve those boundaries and keep current records distinguishable from historical ones.

## Recommended implementation priorities

1. Build an advisor-facing progress view from the authoritative program evaluation and its requirement evidence; show curriculum/catalog context, evaluation date, exceptions, and the source of each credit.
2. Join degree-plan items to actual course/term offerings, registration eligibility, and learner availability/preferences. Surface unavailable or blocked requirements with the responsible office or next action rather than silently substituting excess electives.
3. Use effective-dated support assignments, early-alert activities, communication history, and referral outcomes to drive a documented follow-up queue. Add separate staffing and caseload-capacity measures before using the data to claim high-touch coverage.
4. Govern sensitive support data and predictive scores with role-based access, data freshness indicators, explainable model context, human review, and recurring subgroup and first-term performance checks.

**Conclusion:** GUIDE contains strong structural foundations for clearer degree planning, transfer-credit traceability, barrier resolution, proactive outreach, and wraparound referrals. Its value to advisors depends on trustworthy integrations, explicit institutional rules, and responsible operational practices, especially for sensitive data and predictive interventions.
